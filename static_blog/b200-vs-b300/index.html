B200 vs B300: which GPU fits your workload

Same generation, different memory ceiling. The spec gap between B200 and B300 — and which workloads actually need the upgrade.

By ICE Castle Infrastructure Team/OCT 13, 2026/4 MIN READ

If your workload is memory-bound — long-context reasoning, mixture-of-experts, high-concurrency production inference — the B300 is built for you. Everything else usually runs just as well, and cheaper per GPU-hour, on the B200.

01 / THE SPEC GAP IS MOSTLY MEMORY

Most public trackers put the B300 (Blackwell Ultra) at roughly 288GB of HBM3e per GPU — about 50% more than the B200’s 192GB. That memory step-up is the one figure nearly every source agrees on; bandwidth and raw FLOPS numbers vary more by tracker. On low-precision (FP4) compute, most trackers report the B300 running 30–50% faster than the B200; FP8 throughput comparisons are less consistent across sources. Power draw follows the same direction: the B200 is usually listed around 1,000W, while B300 listings range from 1,100W to 1,400W depending on the source — so a B300 deployment generally asks for more power and cooling headroom per node.

02 / WHICH ONE FITS YOUR WORKLOAD

The honest answer is: most workloads don’t need the B300. It earns its keep specifically where memory capacity is the bottleneck, not raw compute.

  • B2B200 fits: pretraining, fine-tuning, high-throughput inference, and standard-context RAG — workloads that aren’t memory-bound. Generally the lower cost per GPU-hour of the two, with a smaller power and cooling footprint per node.
  • B3B300 fits: long chain-of-thought reasoning, large-context RAG, mixture-of-experts architectures, and high-concurrency production inference — plus training runs where big activation tensors or gradient accumulation push against memory limits.

Mapped against the workloads we see most: video generation and multimodal pipelines tend to push into B300 territory because of how much context and intermediate state they hold in memory. Standard LLM inference and routine fine-tuning usually run fine on a B200 unless your context windows or concurrency are unusually high.

03 / THE PRICING PICTURE

B300 capacity generally prices at a premium over B200 given the memory and power step-up, but the spread we found across public trackers was wide — anywhere from roughly at-parity to nearly double, depending on provider and tier, and still settling as B300 supply ramps through 2026. 04 / HOW TO SPECIFY YOUR DEPLOYMENT

GPU type is the first of the five variables that move your real rate — see what “qualifying deployments” actually means for the rest. When you request a quote, tell us your context length, concurrency, and whether your pipeline is memory- or compute-bound; that’s usually enough for us to tell you which chip actually fits, instead of defaulting you to whichever one we have more of.

The B300 isn’t a strictly-better B200 — it’s a different trade, and it costs more to make. Spend the extra only where memory is actually your bottleneck. For everything else, the B200 is the workhorse for a reason.
